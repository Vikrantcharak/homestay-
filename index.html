Ravi Valley · below Bharmour · Chamba · Himachal Pradesh

Three minutes from the road, you cross the river.

Everything after that is the far side. Along the bank, up into the forest, and half an hour later there are two houses in a clearing — one built the old way, one new. No road reaches them. There is no third house.

01Getting here

The river comes first. The walk comes after.

Most people expect it the other way round. Here is exactly what the last hour involves, so nothing on the day is a surprise.

You leave the vehicle on the road above the river. Three minutes down a steep path and you are at the water.

There is no bridge at this point. There is a cable strung across the Ravi and a box that hangs from it, and you pull yourself over hand over hand. Everyone here calls it the gharuru. It is how rations, tools, timber, children and goats have crossed this stretch for as long as anyone remembers. We come to the road and cross with you — we work the rope, we carry the bags.

Then the long part. The path runs along the Ravi for a while with the water on your left, then turns up into the forest. Depending on your pace and your bag it is thirty to fifty minutes before two slate roofs appear in a clearing.

Nothing motorised comes past that river. That is not a shortcoming of the place — it is the reason to come.

Both houses are ours. There is no third.

No neighbours, no shop, no traffic, no loudspeaker. The forest, the field, the river below, and whoever you came with.

If you have been looking for somewhere to be genuinely unreachable for a few days, this is what that actually looks like.

02Where you sleep

One old house, one new one

Take whichever suits you. Take both if you are a group — you would have the whole clearing to yourselves.

The old house

Built the way houses were built here

Stone, timber and slate in the old Gaddi manner — thick walls, low doorways, small windows made for winters rather than views. Cool through the summer afternoons, and it holds its heat at night.

03What there is to do

Nothing is scheduled. Plenty is possible.

Nobody will knock on your door with an itinerary. When you want something, ask.

Fire in the front yard

There is a clear patch in front of the houses made for exactly this. Wood we can sort out. Sit as late as you like — there is no one to disturb.

The terrace, after dark

Climb up and lie down. On a clear night the Milky Way is not something you have to look for. Bring a blanket in any season.

Stone work by the river

The Ravi bank is all flat, glacier-ground stone. Balance them, stack them, build something the next flood will take away. Hours disappear into this.

A day in the field

We farm, and you are welcome in it. Sow, weed, harvest, or just carry your own vegetables back up to the kitchen. Real work, at whatever pace you like.

Walk out further

Ridges, forest paths, shepherd routes, waterfalls. Some are an afternoon, some a full day. Tell us how far you want to go and we'll point you at the right one — or come with you.

Cook, or don't

The kitchen is yours if you want it. If you'd rather not, we can arrange someone to cook for you for the length of your stay — say so when you book.

04After the sun goes behind the ridge

The only thing switched on is the sky

There is no streetlight between this clearing and the ridge line. No headlights, no shop sign, no other house.

It takes most people a full day to stop listening for traffic that is never coming. Light the fire, eat late, then take the stairs to the terrace and give your eyes twenty minutes.

05Food

It comes from the field below the house

We grow what we eat, and no pesticide goes on any of it. What ends up on the plate depends on the month — that is the honest version, and it is the reason it tastes the way it does.

Meals are simple Gaddi and Chamba home cooking, not restaurant food. If there is something you cannot eat, tell us before you arrive so we can plan around it. There is no shop within walking distance.

06The year here

Five different places, depending when you come

Melt

Mar – Apr

The valley greens up fast. Blossom in the orchards, high water in the Ravi, and nights that still need a jacket.

Long light

May – Jun

Warmest and clearest. The best window for the high walks, and the easiest time to arrive with a heavy bag.

Rain

Jul – Aug

Everything is loud and green. The path gets slippery and the river runs hard. Manimahesh fills with pilgrims in Bhadon; up here it stays empty.

Harvest

Sep – Oct

The clearest skies of the year and the best month on the terrace. Work in the fields if you want a hand in it.

Cold

Nov – Feb

Snow on the ridges and short, sharp days. Winter stays are by arrangement — message us before you plan one.

07Around here

Halfway between the temples and the pass

At Kharamukh the Budhil meets the Ravi. Left up the Budhil goes to Bharmour; right up the Ravi goes to Holi. You are on that second road, which puts most of the valley within a day of the house.

08Before you come

The things worth knowing in advance

We would rather you arrive prepared than arrive disappointed.

Pack so you can carry it

A rucksack, not a wheeled suitcase. We help with bags, but the last stretch is a footpath and a river crossing — whatever you bring, someone carries.

The crossing needs two working hands

The gharuru is safe and we operate it, but you do step in and out of a hanging box over moving water. If anyone in your group has a balance or mobility concern, message us before booking and we will talk it through honestly.

Phone signal and power

Signal and power in the valley change with the season and the weather. Ask us for the current picture before you come, and plan to be offline for some of your stay.

Nights are cold, always

Even in June. The valley loses its heat the moment the sun goes behind the ridge. Carry one more layer than you think you need.

Carry cash

No card machine here and no ATM near the road end. Bring enough cash for your whole stay.

Shoes, and a torch

Grippy closed shoes for the descent and the riverbank. A headtorch is worth more than you would think once the fire dies down.

This place suits

  • People who want quiet more than they want convenience
  • Walkers, birders, writers, anyone with a project
  • Trekkers using the valley as a base
  • Families with children old enough to walk an hour

This place does not suit

  • Anyone who needs a vehicle at the door
  • Anyone who needs to be reachable at all times
  • Very short stops — one night barely covers the walk
  • Anyone looking for a party venue

09Questions

Asked before almost every booking

How do I get to the road end?

Most guests come up through Chamba town and along the Ravi valley road to Kharamukh, then take the Holi road. The nearest railway station is Pathankot and the nearest airport is Gaggal, near Dharamshala. Tell us where you are starting from and we will send directions and fix a time to meet you at the road.

Is the gharuru crossing safe?

Yes. It is how people here have crossed this stretch for generations, and we operate it for you and carry your bags. You do step in and out of a hanging box over moving water, so if anyone in your group has a balance or mobility concern, message us before booking and we will talk it through honestly.

Can we bring children?

Yes, if they can manage a walk of about an hour on a forest path. The crossing and the walk are part of the adventure for most kids.

How many nights should we stay?

Two at the very least. One night barely covers the walk in and out. Most people find three or four is when the quiet properly sinks in.

What about food?

Meals are simple Gaddi and Chamba home cooking from our own pesticide-free field. You can also use the kitchen yourself, or ask for someone to cook for your whole stay. There is no shop within walking distance, so tell us about any dietary needs before you arrive.

Can we take one house, or both?

Either. Groups often take both and have the whole clearing to themselves. Mention which you'd like when you message us.
